Why this started

A main road, a light sleeper, and a pattern I couldn't ignore

I live next to a main road in the suburbs of Seattle. Late at night, cars sometimes race through the street at high speeds — engines revving, tires cutting through the quiet. At first it just annoyed me: it woke my family and pulled my focus off my work. Then I learned Snohomish County records the highest rate of traffic casualties in Washington State, with over 400 crashes here in the last ten years. This stopped being an annoyance and started looking like a public safety and public health issue.

I began researching neighborhood road safety efforts across the country and found others tracking speeding with simple sensors and sound data. That led to a research project analyzing six years of Snohomish County traffic data — and eventually, to speaking directly with the county about what the data shows.

Now I'm going door to door in my own neighborhood, because the data is only half the argument. The other half is knowing how many of us are actually losing sleep over it.

The data behind Gerald

Six years of county traffic data, four findings

Every stat below comes from Snohomish County Public Works traffic counts, Washington Traffic Safety Commission fatality records, and vehicle noise data, analyzed alongside my research partner Swarali Karale in our paper, The Impacts of Speeding on Noise Levels and Public Safety in Snohomish County.

4–10 dB

over the WHO nighttime noise limit

SnoCo's average nighttime road noise consistently exceeds the WHO's 45 dB guideline — and every 4 dB roughly doubles perceived loudness.

12 – 3 AM

is when speeding outliers peak

When roads are clearest, speeds vary the most. Drivers going well above the limit are most common exactly when the neighborhood is trying to sleep.

~32%

less sound energy at −15 mph

Our machine learning model simulated a 15 mph reduction in speed limits county-wide and found it could cut total sound energy by roughly a third.

94.3%

of local vehicles are gas-powered

Gas vehicles dominate both SnoCo's vehicle mix and its noise contribution, especially as speed increases.
